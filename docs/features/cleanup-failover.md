# Optional cleanup backups — source experiment

Cleanup backups are off unless a complete ordered chain is explicitly reviewed and confirmed in AI Cleanup. The primary's canonical endpoint, exact effective model, credential precedence and access class are included in that review. Each of at most two backups has its own exact provider, endpoint, model and selected credential source: named environment variable, scoped Keychain key, or explicitly keyless compatible access. A missing selected source stops; it never falls through. Credential values are not consent identities. Saving a key does not enable backups. One Keychain entry is shared by models at the same provider and canonical endpoint, including the primary when it uses that entry. Replacing or removing it affects those users of the entry; environment credentials are unchanged. A loopback endpoint still uses network transport and cannot attest that the server does not forward text.

Only ordinary physical keyboard/mouse dictation and ordinary result-only Shortcuts participate. A capture gets an immutable grant before queueing or recognition, bound to its exact operation. Selected presets, selection transforms, history retries, segmented cleanup, evaluation, warm requests and Apple Intelligence keep their existing primitive behavior. Apple never falls back to a network provider. A profile with a different effective cleanup model pauses the chain for that job.

In the Hub, review and confirmation require Dictation output set to Normal cleanup (Output & Presets) and cleanup not turned off in settings. Mode changes save automatically; the chain must be reviewed again after the save. Review and confirmation refuse pending or failed ordinary settings saves. They do not flush them. Disable revokes immediately even if its disk write fails, preserves unrelated pending edits and callbacks, and requires explicit repair/retry to persist a failed disable. Relevant drafts, observed saved changes, invalid authority, disable, quit and restart intent irreversibly revoke old job grants. Reverting values, reconfirming or canceling quit cannot revive an old grant. A primary already running retains frozen intent under operation/deadline ownership; only backup authority is revoked. Active backups are canceled and joined through actual owned exit. Quit/restart participation revokes and aborts the whole registered operation, including its primary; canceling that intent cannot revive it. A still-recording successor makes quit/restart refuse until the user finishes or cancels that recording.

One monotonic 15-second cleanup result budget starts when the eligible client is constructed, before its experiment primary credential setup. Equality is expired. Under executor progress a retained watchdog selects raw text plus existing local rules once at expiry, independently of actual worker exit. Capture, ASR and entry migration/preparation are outside this timer. A backup needs at least three seconds remaining both at admission and actual resume; slow setup consumes the same budget. A slow primary can leave no backup time. The finalizer delivers the logical result then unconditionally drains worker and watchdog before the existing serial queue marks the operation exited. Actual transport/native exit, billing, process termination and queued-successor recognition are not time bounded.

Eligible primaries retain environment → legacy-refusal → scoped-Keychain → compatible-keyless/missing-stop precedence, with noninteractive credential reads and bounded owned HTTP. Credential IO runs outside settings/run locks; its eventual return cannot restore expired authority. Off, paused, ungranted, profile/context and other primitive routes retain their existing defaults. A new digest contract token pauses old reviews while preserving Settings decoding; review and confirm again.

Only actual B21 participation registers the narrow busy owner. New ordinary keyboard, mouse and Shortcuts starts refuse before preemption, credential work or generation advance until worker and watchdog exit. Disabling settings cannot bypass the owner. An already recording successor keeps its PCM; on stop the existing queue retains it, shows waiting feedback and permits explicit discard. A deadline result from the old operation cannot change that newer capture. A finite queued prefix can already exist at registration. These recordings can wait indefinitely behind uncooperative actual exit; no additional scheduler or native overlap is introduced.

Only typed transient URL failures and HTTP 408, 429 and 500–599 can advance the chain. Recognized authentication, security, configuration, refusal and content categories outrank transient status, including expired credentials at any HTTP status. Unknown errors, cancellation, deadline, TLS, redirects, empty/corrupt/truncated/oversized responses and invalid Pipeline output stop. Every attempt receives the same immutable transcript and context. Pipeline sanitizes and validates one candidate, then applies literal rules once; it never shops for another result after validation rejects one. Locked snippets contact no provider.

Eligible primary and backups reuse the bounded B30 transport: 16 KiB prompt, 128 KiB request body, 256 KiB response, 16 KiB received headers, 16 KiB explicit outgoing headers including Authorization, and a 1 MiB payload ledger. Overlong prompts stop without truncation. Suspended canceled handles still join completion and exit. No ambient cookies, cache, credentials, redirect following, authentication retry or model discovery is added. These payload caps do not prove total RSS or Foundation/native allocation bounds. B30's existing 60-second default remains unchanged.

Diagnostics retain the configured primary separately from at most three finite attempt records, request starts, terminal outcomes, candidate and accepted producer ordinals. Acceptance is finalized only after Pipeline validation, external backup-grant checks and an atomic local deadline/latch recheck. Evidence and producer metrics freeze at logical selection; late adapter callbacks cannot change diagnostics, global Metrics, delivery or history. Eligible adapters do not receive granular DiagnosticOperation forwarding; the outer Pipeline wrapper and finite owned attempt records supply evidence. Model observations use finite enums; endpoints, arbitrary model/error strings, keys, transcript and review digests are not exported. History uses operation-owned producer metrics, including an explicit empty value, so it cannot borrow another job's global tokens. Experimental primary/backup labels are unverified and unpriced. Reported producer usage is not all-attempt spend; remote billing may be unknown.

The settings digest detects staleness in user-editable JSON; it is not authentication. App locks and fresh saved-byte checks order observed app actions, not arbitrary filesystem editors or remote acknowledgements. Existing secure-input, destination, stale-generation, History-off, Original/Copy and result-only guards remain independent of cleanup success.

The revised macOS experiment meets B21's canonical code/policy and controlled-comparison criteria. Full independent source review approved all thirteen paths; root accepted the scoped behavior on 2026-10-31. Current checks cover 226 focused methods, two serial fifteen-second default-sleep observations, and one broad run of 1,750 methods with five opt-in native/elapsed skips. Only the two previously excluded real-model tests were omitted. See `docs/qualification/macos/2026-10-31-cleanup-failover.md` for acceptance evidence and limits.

Controlled fake-provider comparisons establish behavior and extra work only. Real providers, credentials, native UI, billing, quality and usefulness remain unqualified; no improvement in those properties is claimed. The cleanup deadline is a logical result bound under executor progress, not a universal OS realtime or actual-exit bound. A nonblocking test-coverage nit remains: the slow-watchdog test proves eventual drain but lacks a held-watchdog noncompletion assertion. Source explicitly joins the watchdog; connected held-worker checks establish the separate queue barrier. The historical allocator abort mechanism remains unestablished.
